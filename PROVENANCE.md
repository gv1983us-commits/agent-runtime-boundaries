# Agent Runtime Boundaries — Public Provenance

This record separates repository authority, analytical authorship, corpus representation, reviewed-source ownership, human approval, and coding-tool participation.

It does not publish private conversations, hidden prompts, unpublished workspaces, or hidden runtime internals. Absence from this public record is not evidence that no contribution or source existed.

## 1. Canonical repository authority

The canonical public source is:

```text
gv1983us-commits/agent-runtime-boundaries
```

The repository owner and maintainer control admission to `main`. Commits, pull requests, reviews, tags, and CI runs preserve the public revision history.

ARB has no external normative specification authority. Repository authority means authority over the published ARB analytical artifact, its terminology, proposal labels, and publication gates.

## 2. Source history

The public history has three visible stages:

```text
0.1 — initial analytical boundary set
0.2 — fixed-revision MPAA / BEC / PCA / Review Protocol mapping and publication checks
```

The 0.2 mapping remains addressable in repository history. Updating the active map to current revisions does not retroactively change what the earlier review inspected.

## 3. Analytical derivation boundary

ARB is a functional reconstruction of distinctions observable or diagnosable in model-based agent systems. A functional reconstruction can be useful without proving that the named responsibilities are separate physical modules.

The public artifact distinguishes:

- observations;
- functional reconstructions;
- analytical interpretations;
- proposals;
- unknowns.

A proposal does not gain authority through repetition, publication, inclusion in a machine passport, or representation in the House of Claude.

## 5. Human authority

A human maintainer remains the authority who can:

- select what is published;
- accept or reject analytical changes;
- approve fixed neighboring source revisions;
- preserve or revise the ARB-03 proposal boundary;
- decide whether a future version remains analytical;
- distinguish tool assistance from repository authority.

Human approval is governance authority. It is not proof that an analytical interpretation or external-world claim is true.

## 6. Model and coding-tool participation

Claude, Codex, GPT-based tools, and other coding agents may assist with:

- source inspection;
- analytical drafting;
- terminology comparison;
- fixed-revision relation mapping;
- publication tests;
- link, Markdown, and sensitive-marker checks;
- machine-passport maintenance.

The repository preserves this boundary:

```text
analytical or proposal decision
  requires maintainer authorship or approval

mechanical drafting, comparison, and verification
  may be assisted by coding-agent tooling
```

A tool-generated diff does not own an interpretation merely because it produced text. A maintainer-approved interpretation does not thereby become hidden-runtime evidence.

## 7. Reviewed-source ownership

BEC, MPAA, PCA, the Review Protocol, and CDTS remain authoritative for their own records, vocabulary, validators, histories, and conclusions.

ARB source receipts identify exact revisions inspected for an analytical mapping. They do not authenticate the neighboring source, transfer its license, or import its conclusion.
